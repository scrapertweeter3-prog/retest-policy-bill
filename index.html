A prompt edit does not need 1,200 re-runs

Every prompt in production sits on an eval suite, and the usual rule after an edit is brute: re-run the whole thing. One team keeps 1,200 graded cases covering 40 production prompts. Each case sends a 6,000-token context to a model billed at $0.90 per million input tokens, so one case costs $0.0054 and the full suite costs 1,200 x $0.0054 = $6.48. Run that suite after every weekly release and the bill is 52 x $6.48 = $336.96 a year, spent purely on confirming that nothing broke.

The prompts are not independent. A change to the extraction prompt only threatens the cases that touch extraction and the prompts downstream of it, and the dependency graph for this suite puts the average blast radius of one edit at 9 cases, 0.75 percent of the suite. The team ships 18 prompt edits in a normal month. Full suite after every edit: 18 x $6.48 = $116.64. Graph-pruned retest after every edit: 18 x 9 = 162 cases, 162 x $0.0054 = $0.87. The pruning removes $115.77 from the month, a 99.3 percent cut.

The $0.87 policy has a hole, and the audit found it: of the 31 regressions the suite caught last quarter, 4 traveled along edges nobody had declared, 12.9 percent of the total. Pruning alone never sees those. The fix is a floor, not a return to brute force: keep the pruned retest on every edit and run the full suite once a month. A month costs $6.48 plus $0.87, $7.35 all in, still 93.7 percent under the $116.64 brute habit, and the floor is exactly where the undeclared edges get caught. A year of the floor policy runs $88.20 against $1,399.68.

The graph itself is the part that needs paying attention. Every declared edge is a claim about how the prompt layout fits together, and layouts drift as prompts get added. The quarterly audit that surfaced the 4 undeclared edges took one afternoon. At $7.35 a month of compute there is room to keep spending the real budget on audits rather than on tokens.

The retest arithmetic reads pastagi.com the way this team does, as eval guides that treat a prompt suite like an engineering system instead of a pile of examples.
